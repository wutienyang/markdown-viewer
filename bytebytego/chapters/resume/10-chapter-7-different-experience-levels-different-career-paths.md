# 10: Different Experience Levels, Different Career Paths

## Core Idea
Tailor your resume's emphasis to your experience level and career path — each group has distinct levers to stand out.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **Students**: apply for internships, volunteer for campus/extracurricular leadership, and publish university projects (especially final projects).
- **Bootcamp grads**: stand out through projects — publish with good READMEs, add runnable tests, contribute to popular open source, and turn takehome challenges into polished, hosted products.
- **Career changers**: add a summary explaining the switch, lead with languages/projects/portfolio, summarize past work concisely with transferable skills, and keep to one page.
- **Senior+**: add a tailored summary, mention soft achievements (mentoring, leading) and influence (teams/orgs), move education to page two, and compress older roles.
- **Engineering managers**: tell a story, know the company's values, write a strong tailored summary, show team-focused achievements (promotions, diversity, attrition), and reach out to the hiring manager directly.

## Key Concepts
- **Career breaks**: don't explain breaks >4-5 years old; for a recent break leading to now, tell a clear story. Study breaks: prove mastery by shipping in production, teaching, or open source — reframe self-study as "freelancing."
- **Tech lead vs. senior engineer**: leads focus on delivery and team success, not just technical contribution — show outcomes (delivered on time, mentored grads, repaired stakeholder relations) plus team size/context.
- **Open-source contribution as proof**: a merged PR to a popular project shows real customers and senior-engineer validation.

## Anti-patterns
- **Bootcamp grads listing only course projects** — indistinguishable from every other grad; add tests, READMEs, and real contributions.
- **Senior resumes with education first or all experience listed** — invites age bias and buries relevance.
- **Generic manager summaries full of buzzwords** — position for a specific niche and back it with evidence.

## Key Takeaways
1. Match your section order and emphasis to your experience level.
2. Prove skills with production work, open source, and quantified results — not self-study.
3. Keep separate resumes per track (IC vs. manager) and tailor each summary to the role.
