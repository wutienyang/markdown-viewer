# 03: Why Resumes and CVs are Important

## Core Idea
The resume's only goal is to get you an interview for that specific position — in under 10 seconds — not to document your whole career.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **Goal of a resume**: get you to an interview for that position; sell you for that job in the ~7-10 seconds a recruiter scans.
- **Yes/Maybe/No model**: hiring managers triage piles of resumes fast — aim to "jump out" as a Yes candidate.
- **Good vs. Great resumes**: Good = represents you fairly. Great = showcases rare, exactly-relevant experience; greatness is about content, not format.
- **Career progression matters**: a trail of promotions and growing responsibility stands in contrast to a "journeyman" resume (zero visible progression).

## Key Concepts
- **Journeyman resume**: a story with little or no career progression — often a rejection reason at high-growth companies.
- **Recruiter scan time**: ~7 seconds on average to decide reject vs. read further.
- **LinkedIn-as-resume**: fine when you're in demand, poor for direct applications (can't tailor, no versioning, verbose, lock-in).

## Anti-patterns
- **Underselling strong experience**: even Google/Apple experience needs selling; structure past roles to showcase your best personal contributions.
- **Relying on LinkedIn exports**: shows you're not really interested; use a tailored resume instead.
- **Believing format is the only blocker**: a good resume is required but not sufficient — competition, visa, location, seniority, and luck all matter.

## Key Takeaways
1. Write the resume to sell you for one job, not to be a complete, accurate history.
2. Make career progression and your best contributions visible on first scan.
3. Build and customize a real resume; keep LinkedIn updated as a separate sourcing tool.
