# 01: Introduction

## Core Idea
A developer resume's job is to represent you fairly and get you a recruiter call; the book teaches you to write one deliberately for the people who read it.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **No one-size-fits-all**: tailor your resume to the country, industry, and company; do your own research and get feedback from your network.
- **Write for the reader**: know the hiring process and who reads your resume before you write it.
- **Skim → template → rewrite**: skim the book once, pick one or two templates, then rewrite your resume while doing the Part 2 exercises.

## Key Concepts
- **FAANG**: Facebook, Apple, Amazon, Netflix, Google — top-paying, most competitive tech employers.
- **Before/after examples**: anonymized real resume snippets with improvement areas highlighted.
- **"From the inside out"**: first-person insights from hiring managers and recruiters on how and why things are done.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming an easy job market**: outside senior/staff roles at known companies, direct applications are competitive.
- **Treating the book as gospel for any specific company**: hiring differs per company and even per hiring manager.
- **Expecting a formula**: the book won't explain why you didn't hear back — factors like competition and timing are unpredictable.

## Key Takeaways
1. The goal is a fair resume that maximizes your chance of passing the screen, assuming you're qualified.
2. Know the audience (recruiters, sourcers, hiring managers) before writing.
3. Use templates and exercises to write and rewrite, not to follow one "magic" format.
