# 16: Resume Improvement Examples

## Core Idea
Real before/after resumes show the same recurring fixes: easy-to-read templates, quantified impact, active language, tailoring, and removing noise — use them as inspiration, never copy.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **Recurring fixes**: easy-to-read single-column template, numbers and impact, active verbs, tailored languages/technologies, location listed, education dates present, PDF format, promotions visible, drop irrelevant old roles.
- **Draw focus deliberately**: guide attention to titles/dates/results, not company-name links or scattered URLs.
- **One page when possible**: a 3-page ML resume refactored to one page got more leads; even a 20-year SRE fits two pages by cutting old roles.

## Key Concepts
- **Location matters**: omit it and recruiters may assume you're abroad (and need a visa).
- **Self-ratings hurt**: a candidate rating themselves 4/5 Python, 3/5 JS read as weak — remove ratings entirely.
- **Promotion visibility**: "10 years with no advancement" reads poorly; surface promotions to show progression.

## Anti-patterns
- **Dumping all technologies** (bolding everything, "W4 forms") — draws attention to the wrong areas.
- **Listing old, irrelevant roles** (pre-MSc jobs, non-tech positions) — dilutes the story.
- **Copying examples one-for-one** — they're someone else's experience; extract the improvement patterns instead.

## Key Takeaways
1. Apply the same core fixes (template, impact numbers, active voice, tailoring) across every resume.
2. Always list location and education dates; show promotions and remove obsolete roles.
3. Use the examples as a checklist for your own resume, not as templates to copy.
