# 07: Resume Structure

## Core Idea
Order sections by experience level — new grads lead with internships/projects/education, experienced people lead with work experience — and make the resume tell a story.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **New grad / bootcamp priority order**: real-world dev experience → internships → sizable open-source contributions → school details → standout projects → tutoring/leadership.
- **With work experience**: work experience near the top; a Languages & Technologies section on page one; promotions shown as sub-sections; education shrinks with seniority; compress or drop >10-year-old roles.
- **Tell a story backward**: make promotions visible, clarify non-standard titles, drop months from old dates, omit experience that doesn't support your story.
- **Languages & Technologies approaches**: (1) separate section; (2) weave technologies into each position (conveys recency); (3) split out rusty/not-hands-on tech. Avoid listing expertise levels.

## Key Concepts
- **Summary section**: rarely read on first scan; keep it short and tailored — useful for senior/standout profiles, remote-only seekers, and role changes (manager→IC). Write it last.
- **Title adjustments**: ethical only when your actual responsibilities (3+ months) don't match your title, or the title is company-specific.
- **Separate resumes per track**: for experienced engineers, keep distinct manager and IC resumes telling different stories — never contradict facts.

## Anti-patterns
- **Summary statements that oversell** (e.g., "looking to lead") can get you rejected; only add specifics that fit the role.
- **Listing expertise levels or points** on languages/technologies; use listing order to signal comfort instead.
- **Listing every old role** (internships a decade ago, non-technical jobs); your resume should sell you, not log your life.

## Key Takeaways
1. Put the most role-relevant sections at the top, first page, based on your experience level.
2. Make promotions and progression obvious; clarify titles when your actual role differs.
3. Add a tailored summary only when it helps (senior, remote, or changing roles); otherwise skip it.
