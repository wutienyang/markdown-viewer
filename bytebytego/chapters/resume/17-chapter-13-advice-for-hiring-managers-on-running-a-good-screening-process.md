# 17: Advice for Hiring Managers on Running a Good Screening Process

## Core Idea
Resume screening always rejects good people with poor resumes — so managers must invest in a fairer process, partner with recruitment, write good job descriptions, and build feedback loops.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **Know screening is broken**: accept you'll reject good candidates; use Yes/Maybe/No and re-read the Maybe pile before filling the role.
- **Treat recruitment as a partner**: build relationships with recruiters/sourcers, explain what makes people successful, empower them to champion standouts, and review resumes yourself.
- **Write a good job description**: challenge what's truly required (language is often negotiable), and de-bias wording to widen the applicant pool.
- **Have a feedback loop**: monitor pipeline and diversity metrics, run retrospectives, and set a clear policy on giving feedback at each stage.

## Key Concepts
- **Assessment-based screening**: platforms (Woven, Triplebyte, Hackerrank) replace or supplement resume screening and recover "hidden gems" you'd have rejected.
- **Hackerrank caution**: build challenges that mirror real day-to-day work; don't over-index on algorithm scores or reject on one missed edge case.
- **Self-selection bias**: you'll favor people like yourself — acknowledge it and screen for diverse profiles.
- **BusyManager vs. MakeTimeManager**: results track how much time and partnership the manager invests, not the recruiters used.

## Anti-patterns
- **Rigid must-haves** (exact years/language) that reject otherwise strong candidates; negotiate sensible guidelines instead.
- **Copy-pasting job descriptions** or using masculine wording that turns away applicants.
- **Hiring managers working in silos** from HR/recruitment — a missed opportunity for better results.

## Key Takeaways
1. Involve a second reviewer, budget dedicated time, and revisit the Maybe pile to reduce false negatives.
2. Empower recruiters to take chances on enthusiastic, atypical candidates.
3. Track pipeline diversity and outcomes, and retrospect after both successful and failed onsites.
