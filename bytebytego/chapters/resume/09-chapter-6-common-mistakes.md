# 09: Common Mistakes

## Core Idea
Most rejections come from a handful of repeat mistakes: poor formatting, forgetting the audience, unnecessary details, and links that draw attention away from your content.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **Poor format**: hard-to-scan multi-column layouts, too much or inconsistent bolding, "flashy" designs, inconsistent font/alignment, sloppy phrases ("etc.", slang). Simple single-column works best.
- **Forgetting the audience**: avoid internal acronyms/jargon, tailor to the position (lead with frontend examples for a frontend role), replace cliches with evidence ("organized two offsites" > "team player"), and cut verbosity.
- **Unnecessary details to cut**: photos, more than four contact/social links, spoken languages, self-rated skills, "references available on request", and quotes from others.
- **Links**: make them clickable, hide long URLs behind descriptive names, only link to up-to-date content, and make them blend in (no default blue) rather than stand out.

## Key Concepts
- **Evidence over cliche**: back every claim with a concrete example from your work.
- **Attention budget**: use color and bolding only to direct the reader to titles, dates, and key results — not to links or random words.
- **Stale links**: untouched GitHub, dead websites, or outdated LinkedIn undermine trust; omit links if they don't add value.

## Anti-patterns
- **Bolding random mid-sentence words** and inconsistent bolding — signals low attention to detail.
- **Including spoken languages / references / self-ratings** that only waste space or invite bias.
- **Long pasted URLs and blue link colors** that steal the reader's first-scan attention.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use a single-column, consistent template; bold only titles, companies, and dates.
2. Remove photos, extra contact details, spoken languages, and reference sections.
3. Make every link clickable, relevant, current, and visually muted; verify each one.
