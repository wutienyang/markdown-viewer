# 04: The Hiring Pipeline

## Core Idea
Understand who reads your resume and how the pipeline drops off at each stage, so you can prioritize referrals, tailor applications, and target the right companies.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **Roles**: hiring manager (final decision), recruiter (fills headcount), sourcer (outreach), inbound sourcer (screens applications), coordinator (logistics), interview panel (keeps the bar fair).
- **Pipeline stages**: ATS queue → resume/CV screen → recruiter screen → technical screen → onsite → offer. Dropoff is steepest at the resume screen.
- **Referrals skip the queue**: referrals almost always guarantee an in-depth review and often a recruiter call; strong referrals (someone who's worked with you) can set up a call directly.
- **Resume priority order**: referrals → local candidates → no-visa-needed → visa+relocation → difficult-visa countries.

## Key Concepts
- **ATS (Applicant Tracking System)**: a coordination tool for recruiters; humans — not the ATS — reject resumes.
- **Referrals vs. references vs. background checks**: referrals are internal recommendations (before applying); references are post-interview checks; background checks come after the offer.
- **Knockout/prescreening questions**: the one real "ATS rejection" — set by recruiters (e.g., visa sponsorship), filled from the application form, not the resume.
- **Job aggregators vs. job boards**: aggregators (Indeed, SimplyHired, CareerJet, LinkUp) crawl career pages and surface low-competition "hidden" jobs.

## Anti-patterns
- **Believing ATS myths**: "ATS rejects 75% of resumes," "PDF gets removed," "ATS-compatible resumes" are false; don't pay to "beat the ATS."
- **Applying before asking for a referral**: once you apply, you're in the system and often can't be referred.
- **Expecting visa sponsorship as a new grad**: rare; sponsorship mostly opens up after 2-3 years of experience.

## Key Takeaways
1. Ask for referrals before applying — they're the biggest lever to get a human to read your resume.
2. Tailor your resume to make the fit obvious in seconds to the recruiter/inbound sourcer.
3. Browse job aggregators and small companies to find lower-competition pipelines; track and prioritize your applications.
