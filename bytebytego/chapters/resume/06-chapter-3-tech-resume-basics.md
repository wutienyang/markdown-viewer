# 06: Tech Resume Basics

## Core Idea
Recruiters scan for five things on autopilot; follow the unwritten ground rules so they find them instantly and don't reject you on trivia or bias.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **The five first-glance facts**: years of experience, relevant technologies, work experience with measured impact, work authorization/visa status, and anything that clearly stands out.
- **Ground rules**: good grammar and no typos; basic contact details; dates in reverse chronological order; no photos or non-required personal info; two pages or less.
- **Simplicity & consistency**: clear consistent formatting, bullet points (no sub-bullets or dash bullets), easy-to-read dates, PDF format, concise text.

## Key Concepts
- **Levels**: job listings map to internal levels (e.g., Google L5 ≈ Facebook L5 ≈ Amazon L6 ≈ Microsoft 63-65); recruiters compare your years to the level.
- **Quantified impact**: visitors, RPS, improvement %, etc., translate across employers better than qualitative descriptions.
- **Bias via personal details**: age, gender, photos, kids, nationality, religion — every extra detail adds a bias risk; you don't need more than name and email.

## Anti-patterns
- **Photos and personal data** (date of birth, gender, citizenship, marital status, religion): trigger unconscious bias; skip them for US-based tech roles.
- **Self-rating expertise** ("expert"/"proficient" labels or point scales): implies weakness elsewhere and invites grilling; list only what you're proficient in, strongest first.
- **Non-PDF formats** (.doc, .rtf): display inconsistently; use `{yourname}_resume.pdf`.

## Key Takeaways
1. Put years, technologies, work experience, and (if applicable) work authorization on page one where they're impossible to miss.
2. Use PDF, one level of bullet points, easy dates ("June 2011–July 2012"), and consistent formatting.
3. Remove all non-essential personal details that can only introduce bias.
