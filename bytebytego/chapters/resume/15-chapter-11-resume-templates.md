# 15: Resume Templates

## Core Idea
Start from a tried-and-proven template and customize it; content matters more than the template, and most generic resume generators are poor fits for developers.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **Criteria for a decent generator/site**: templates optimized for hiring managers, real hiring-manager testimonials, real successful resume examples, clear pricing, and no ATS myths.
- **Recommended templates**: The Pragmatic Engineer's, Standard Resume Parker, Mono Engineer's, Markdown-to-PDF, Experienced Engineer's, Standard Resume Venables, CareerCup.
- **Avoid these**: Google Docs Swiss, resume.io software/web developer templates, EnhanCV, Europass — mostly for two-column layouts, wasted space, or bias-inducing fields.

## Key Concepts
- **Standard Resume** (Parker, Venables): built by developers for developers — single-column, designer spacing, web version + view tracking, and real resumes from people hired at top companies.
- **Points/star rating systems**: self-scores work against you — low scores suggest weakness, high scores are assumed anyway, and self-ratings are unreliable; drop them.
- **CareerCup template**: familiar to US recruiters, easy dates, but dated black-and-white style and crowds content.
- **Europass**: not required for EU jobs; encourages photos/birth dates/gender — avoid it.

## Anti-patterns
- **Generic resume generators** (resume.io, EnhanCV, VisualCV): two-column layouts, designer-written (not engineer) content, photos, and skills sections that fail the "buzzword" test.
- **Skill point systems** and star ratings — only work against you.
- **Europass CV** — bias-inducing fields and noise (spoken languages, driving licenses).

## Key Takeaways
1. Pick a single-column, developer-proven template and customize colors/fonts, not structure.
2. Prefer templates with real hiring-manager validation and real success examples.
3. Never self-rate skills; list only what you're proficient with, strongest first.
