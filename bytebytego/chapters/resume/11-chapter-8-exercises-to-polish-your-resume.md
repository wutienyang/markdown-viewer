# 11: Exercises to Polish Your Resume

## Core Idea
After drafting your master resume, run through concrete exercises — rewrite from a blank template, hunt down impact numbers, and get grammar and human feedback — to sharpen it.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **Write two very different resumes**, then combine the best parts of each into a third — you'll have a better resume and more material to customize per job.
- **Find the impact of past projects**: ask your team/manager for the current position, reach out to old colleagues for past ones, and estimate a close-enough approximation if you can't be exact.
- **Do a grammar check, not just a spellcheck**: use Grammarly (grammar) and Hemingway Editor (readability), then re-read manually.
- **Keyword check for that position**: compare the job description against your resume; make sure your key terms (e.g., "Java", "backend") appear.

## Key Concepts
- **Fresh eyes**: ask friends/family to proofread — you develop tunnel vision and miss obvious errors.
- **Online feedback**: free forums (r/cscareerquestions, r/resumes, Rands Slack for EMs) — anonymize first and take advice with a grain of salt.
- **ATS scan tools**: use sparingly; most scores are guesswork and ATSes don't reject resumes — don't buy Premium upsells for tech roles.

## Anti-patterns
- **Endlessly tweaking the same resume** instead of starting a genuinely different second version.
- **Listing responsibilities without impact** because you never actually looked up the numbers — go find them.
- **Trusting paid "ATS optimization"** that preys on myths; a keyword overlap check is enough.

## Key Takeaways
1. Write two distinct resumes and merge their best parts into a third.
2. Uncover and estimate real impact numbers; "close enough" beats nothing.
3. Run grammar/readability tools, get human proofreading, and verify keyword overlap per role.
