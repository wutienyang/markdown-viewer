# 14: Good Resume Template Principles

## Core Idea
A good template is designed for recruiters and hiring managers — it makes the key facts effortless to find in a few-second scan, so your content actually gets read.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **Read top-to-bottom**: recruiters start at the top and work down; order the most relevant content first.
- **Single-column layout**: top-down formats are easiest to scan; two-column layouts sacrifice readability and discourage detail.
- **Important things first**: put years, technologies, titles/companies, and location where they can be found at a glance.
- **Strategic color and bolding**: use both consistently to guide the eye to titles, dates, and key results — not randomly.

## Key Concepts
- **First-scan facts**: years of experience and dates, key technologies, titles and companies, and location (local? visa needed?).
- **Top-down vs. two-column**: top-down is optimal for readability and thought-flow; two-column reads like an infographic and only works if you're already great at succinct presentation.
- **Flashy template sites** (EnhanCV, etc.) optimize to sell you the template, not to help recruiters read it — a poor fit for developers.

## Anti-patterns
- **Two-column / multi-column layouts**: harder to scan, columns vary in order, and they encourage buzzwords over impact detail.
- **Cramming content** by shrinking fonts and whitespace — the #1 mistake; readability beats density.
- **"Standout" flashy designs** for non-design roles — they pull attention from content and reduce specifics.

## Key Takeaways
1. Choose a single-column, top-down template that surfaces experience, technologies, and dates immediately.
2. Reserve two-column designs for design/UX roles where the layout is part of the application.
3. Prioritize readability and whitespace over fitting everything on one page.
