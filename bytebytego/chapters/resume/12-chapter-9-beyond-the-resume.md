# 12: Beyond the Resume

## Core Idea
Your LinkedIn, GitHub, technical blog, and cover letters can all get you to a recruiter call — invest in them deliberately and only link to content you're proud of.

## Frameworks & Principles
- **LinkedIn (sourcing) vs. resume (applying)**: optimize your headline and current position for what you want to be found for, not your official title; add keywords recruiters Boolean-search for.
- **GitHub**: only link if there's something worth showing. Optimize with a profile README, pin up to six best repos, and write good descriptions/READMEs.
- **Technical blog**: link to the one or two most relevant articles, described, rather than a bare blog link.
- **Cover letters**: skip for big tech; prioritize personalized letters for small companies/startups or when you can reach the hiring manager directly.

## Key Concepts
- **Boolean search**: recruiters use AND/OR/NOT (e.g., `"React.js" AND "Python"`); include those keywords, closer matches in the headline rank higher.
- **Cover letter basics**: show communication skill, demonstrate role understanding, explain fit, name the company, show you read their site, and attach as PDF.
- **Signal open-to-work** via the Open to Job Opportunities feature, not "Looking for opportunities" in your headline; always keep a current position listed.

## Anti-patterns
- **Empty or stale GitHub** — worse than no link; clean up or omit it.
- **Linking to generic social (Twitter, Instagram, Quora)** — poor at conveying professional value (StackOverflow only if active and relevant).
- **Templated, generic cover letters** — frequently ignored; write a personal, company-first, energetic one when it will actually be read.

## Key Takeaways
1. Align LinkedIn headline/positions with the roles you want, and connect with recruiters to rank higher in searches.
2. Curate GitHub and blog links to your strongest, up-to-date work; link only what sells you.
3. Write tailored cover letters only where they'll be read; a tailored resume already does most of the work.
